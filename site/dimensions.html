Three cyclic exclusions

A spatial triangle realizes the three-way pattern that cannot be arranged along one spatial line.

Construction scaffolding, not fundamental data. The axes, coordinates, and spatial slices show how each order is embedded in a continuum spacetime. The 3D example is a genuine dimension witness: in 1+1 dimensions, null coordinates turn causality into the intersection of two linear orderings; this six-event order requires three, so no rearrangement along one spatial line can preserve all six cross-layer relations and all three diagonal non-relations.

Why those percentages?

The flat-space ordering fraction

Choose two events uniformly from a flat \(D\)-dimensional causal diamond \(I\). They are related when either event lies in the other’s future light cone. If the diamond has volume \(V\), fixing the first event \(x\) leaves a smaller future diamond whose volume scales as its proper-time height to the power \(D\).

This is one equation in two forms, not two different laws. The middle expression counts the available future volume geometrically. The expression on the right is what that integral equals after evaluation.

geometric count   =   evaluated closed form
\[r_D = \frac{2}{V^2}\int_I \mathrm{Vol}\!\left(I(x,q)\right)\,\mathrm{d}^D x = \frac{\Gamma(D+1)\,\Gamma(D/2)}{2\,\Gamma(3D/2)}\]
\(r_D\)Expected fraction of event pairs that are causally related.
\(V\)Total spacetime volume of the causal diamond.
\(I(x,q)\)The smaller causal diamond from \(x\) to the future endpoint \(q\).
\(\Gamma\) · gammaThe gamma function, extending factorials: \(\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!\) for positive integers.

The factor \(2\) counts both possible directions, \(x\prec y\) and \(y\prec x\). Evaluating the powers of interval volume produces gamma functions. The ratio is independent of the diamond’s size because both the numerator and \(V^2\) scale the same way.

\(D=2\)\(\frac{1}{2}\)50%
\(D=3\)\(\frac{8}{35}\)≈ 22.9%
