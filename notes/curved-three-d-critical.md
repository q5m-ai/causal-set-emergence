## 3. A two-sided cut budget from actual equal minima

### Coalescing ties have more structure than arbitrary frozen wells

Take two global minima at state labels zero and d in one fixed smooth chart,
subtracting their common value. Smooth Hermite divided differences give

```math
\begin{aligned}
D(z)&=z^2(z-d)^2G(z),&G&\ge0,\\
\|G\|_{C^2}&\le C\|D\|_{C^6},&
k_0&=d^2G(0),&k_d&=d^2G(d),\\
c_0&=G(0)-2dG'(0)+\tfrac12d^2G''(0).
\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.5}
```

For the uniform bound, express the divided difference with nodes z,0,0,d,d
as the integral of the fourth derivative over the four-simplex. Two z
derivatives require only the sixth derivative. Nonnegativity follows from
that of D, including across the removable roots. On a smaller fixed interior
interval, bounded second derivative and nonnegativity make square root G
Lipschitz. Consequently either both endpoint G values are comparable and
larger than a sufficiently large multiple of d squared, or both are bounded
by that multiple of d squared. The quartic formula and its counterpart at d
then give:

- **Quartic regime:** the stiffnesses and the P values are pairwise comparable,
  and the two natural radii are comparable to d.
- **Flat regime:** both stiffnesses are bounded by a constant times d to power
  four, and both P values by a constant times d squared.

This is an equal-*global*-minimum lemma, not an unproved extension to arbitrary
losing near-minimizers. Terminal direction separation is comparable to d by
the compact actual null flow and its reverse flow; no endpoint-position
inverse is used.

```math
\begin{aligned}
```

```math
b'(a)&=-(R+F(a)+F''(a))v(a).
\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.8}
```

```math
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.9}
```

A smooth radial cutoff, zero for r at most r0/3 and one for r at least

```math

\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.10}
```

This retains **every** minimizing route. At a minimizing support angle the

```math
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.11}
```

On a central target disk the factor is positive and bounded. The critical

## 5. Singular continuous cut curvature is also geometrically realizable

```math
\begin{aligned}

F(u)&=-r(u)\{1-\cos(u-a(u))\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.12}
```

These inputs are smooth. The function a is strictly decreasing, since every
interval contains a positive gap, although a-prime vanishes on C. Its image

This is a **global minimum construction**, not just assigned stationary jets.

Then

```math
\begin{aligned}
\partial_u g_u(v)&=
 \frac{2r(u)a'(u)\sin((v-a(u))/2)\sin((u-v)/2)}
      {\sin((u-a(u))/2)}.
\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.13}
```

Its sign is that of v-u on the chosen intervals. Thus its maximum over u is
attained uniquely at u=v, including when s(v)=0. Consequently F(v) is that
supremum. Construct on a slightly larger secondary interval, multiply the
negative F there by a smooth cutoff equal to one on I, and extend by zero

At p(u)=-r(u)n(a(u)), exactly the two states a(u) and u minimize F+p dot n,
with common value minus r(u). Direct differentiation gives the support-family

```math
\begin{aligned}

F''(u)&=-r(u)\cos(u-a(u))-r(u)a'(u),\\
k_i^{\mathrm{sup}}&=r/2,&k_j^{\mathrm{sup}}&=-ra'/2=(r/2)s.
\end{aligned}\tag{CR3.14}
```

The primary is uniformly nondegenerate. At C all secondary defect jets


Choose the positive r values small enough to stay on the central disk and
