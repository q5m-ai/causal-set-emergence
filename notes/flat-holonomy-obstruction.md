# A complete-action flat holonomy obstruction (#149)

**Written theorem, not a Lean theorem or independent human review.** This
continues the [finite-lift reduction](finite-lift-flat-reduction.md), rather
than stopping at its cubic primitive. A compact flat reflection quotient has
a four-route transition whose **fully matched** primitive contains a quartic
logarithm. The coefficient includes every positive/negative phase sector,
not just the four-positive corner. Its sign is proved by a positive fourth
moment identity. The complete physical action has a nonzero limit in six
dimensions and diverges positively in every dimension at least seven.
The independent flat target is zero.

This is a negative resolution of the proposed **all-dimensional flat G-SS
limit**, not a change to the action or admissibility. The companion's complete
positive theorem for arbitrary flat G-SS regions in dimensions 2--4 remains
valid. This logarithmic mechanism alone leaves dimension five unresolved;
the subsequent [Klein-bottle contact](flat-klein-bottle-obstruction.md)
resolves that dimension negatively. #149/#81/#24 remain open;
the acceptance alternative, coverage and corrected statement are recorded
in section 7. Neither a failed estimate nor an isolated sector is being called
a counterexample.

## 1. An admissible fixed geometry, with no developing-map assumption

For each integer d>=6 put q=d/2 and p=d-4. In Euclidean spatial coordinates
(x,z,y,b), with b in p additional directions, take the group generated by

```math
\begin{aligned}
g_1(x,z,y,b)&=(x+1,z,-y,b),\\
g_2(x,z,y,b)&=(x,z+1,-y,b),\\
g_3(x,z,y,b)&=(x,z,y+10,b),\\
h_j(x,z,y,b)&=(x,z,y,b+10e_j),\qquad 1\le j\le p,\\
X_d&=\mathbb R^{d-1}/\Gamma,\\
M_d&=(-T/2,T/2)\times X_d,\qquad T=4/5,\\
g&=dt^2-h_{\mathrm{flat}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H1}
```

A group element translates x,z by integers, reflects y according to their
sum's parity, and translates y,b by multiples of ten. It is free: a nonzero
x/z shift has no fixed point; with those shifts zero, any remaining nonzero
translation also has none. It is properly discontinuous by these discrete
translation bounds. A fundamental set is `[0,1)^2 x [0,10)^(p+1)`.
Thus X_d is a compact complete smooth flat manifold with its **induced**
volume, of mass 10^(p+1). Spatial orientability is not a G-SS assumption;
the ultrastatic spacetime is time-oriented by dt and is globally hyperbolic.
Completeness and the causal speed bound prove that each time slice is Cauchy.

The open slab is precompact and ambient causally convex by monotonicity of
time. Its only boundary faces are the two smooth compact spacelike Cauchy
slices, achronal with empty joint. Independently, R=0 and J is empty, so its
target is zero. The observable, Poisson law and volume normalization are the
original ones. No quotient-volume formula has yet been substituted into its
kernel.

For an actual causal pair with time gap tau<T<1, each of the x and z target
coordinates has at most two possible integer lifts, necessarily consecutive.
Each choice fixes the reflection parity. Since the other periods are ten,
there is at most one allowed y lift for that parity and at most one in each
b direction. There are therefore at most four causal target lifts. This
finite enumeration is also an immediate concrete instance of FL3.

## 2. The actual four-route transition and its rank defect

Whenever both reflection parities occur, source and target y coordinates
must be near the **same** fixed point of reflection on the circle: 0 or 5.
Indeed both `|W-Y|<T` and `|W+Y|<T` hold after choosing those local coordinates;
the two ten-period choices give just these two centers. Around a simultaneous
x/z antipode write the spatial displacements as `1/2+X`, `1/2+Z`, with
source/target transverse coordinates Y,W relative to that center. The four
squared phases are exactly

```math
\begin{aligned}
s_{\epsilon\eta}
 &=\tau^2-(\epsilon/2+X)^2-(\eta/2+Z)^2
                -(\epsilon\eta W-Y)^2-|b|^2,\\
r&=\tau^2-\tfrac12-X^2-Z^2-Y^2-W^2-|b|^2,\\
u&=-X,\qquad v=-Z,\qquad h=2YW,\\
s_{\epsilon\eta}&=r+\epsilon u+\eta v+\epsilon\eta h,
 \qquad\epsilon,\eta\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H2}
```

Causality of a lift is exactly nonnegativity of its phase; its time direction
is future. At Y=W=0, r=u=v=0, all four lifts are null and no additional lift
can be causal. Their time gap is `sqrt(1/2+|b|^2)`, below T on a nonempty
ball. Their projected null routes are distinct. The four-phase map has rank
four when either Y or W is nonzero, but rank three when both vanish. The
missing coordinate is the actual product h=2YW, not a freely chosen fourth
phase variable. This is a geometric rank defect of a flat manifold, not
curvature focusing.

The linear map from (r,u,v,h) to the four s-coordinates is a Hadamard matrix
of absolute determinant sixteen. In the order `++,+-,-+,--` let
lambda=(1,-1,-1,1). Then h is one quarter of lambda dot s.

### The complete regular complement, including face contacts

We record why this is the only nonregular null mechanism in the slab.
One, two and three null phases have the independence proved in the companion;
four have rank four except at Y=W=0. On the complement of a small
neighborhood of that rank-deficient set the full active phase map and the
physical face coordinates are simultaneously regular. Here are the additional
face checks, not a generic-position assertion.

One constant-time face is independent of the phases because the phases depend
on the time difference, not the common endpoint time. For both faces a
failure would require a nonzero combination of active phase differentials
to have zero spatial components. Put A=sum(lambda_i), E=sum(epsilon_i lambda_i),
F=sum(eta_i lambda_i), B=sum(epsilon_i eta_i lambda_i), using zero coefficients
for inactive lifts. On an independent phase patch A cannot vanish in this
failure, since otherwise the whole phase combination would be zero. The
spatial equations are

```math
\begin{aligned}
AX&=-E/2,& AZ&=-F/2,\\
AY&=BW,& AW&=BY,& Ab&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H3}
```

Together with the active equations in (H2), these give the following complete
list of possible nonzero null time gaps at such a failure:

| Active subset | Consequence of (H3) and the null equations |
| --- | --- |
| One lift | Impossible: a nonzero null covector is not timelike |
| Two adjacent corners | Y=W=0, b=0; gap 1/2 |
| Two diagonal corners, same parity | W=Y or W=-Y as appropriate, X=Z=0, b=0; gap 1/sqrt(2) |
| Three corners | Y=W=X=Z=0, b=0; gap 1/sqrt(2) |
| Four independent corners | No solution; (H3) would force Y=W=0 and destroy independence |

For example, for the three corners other than `--`, their equations give
`u=v=-h`, `r=h`. The y equations in (H3) give Y=W or Y=-W unless both vanish.
In the former case B=A and the x/z equations force h=-1/2, inconsistent with
h=2Y^2. In the latter B=-A and those equations force h=0. Thus both vanish;
the remaining equations give the listed gap. For adjacent corners the x or
z equation fixes the unused displacement to zero, and the y equations plus
the two null equations force Y=W=0 and the gap 1/2. For diagonal corners the
same-parity y displacement vanishes and the two retained x/z displacements
are opposite half-periods. Sign changes of X,Z,W permute the other cases.
If only one parity is available away from the two reflection centers, there
are at most the two diagonal translation lifts, already covered by that row.

Neither listed gap equals T=4/5, which is the time difference when both
physical faces are active. Thus closing face contacts are regular on the
independent-phase complement. At the rank-deficient four-route set a closing
contact with tau=T has nonzero b; the integration below retains it exactly.
Choose the shared short cutoff smaller than 1/2 and the short theorem's
bound. All multiple-route null patches are then entirely long. The ordinary
single-route cutoff contacts are regular as in the companion.

On every compact independent-phase patch, split into the full positive/negative
sign sectors and scale the positive phases by w. The domains are fixed
positive q-balls and negative half-spaces, including the rectified physical
faces. Compact differentiation proves a smooth integer primitive there, to
any fixed finite order. A finite cover of the regular complement gives uniform
bounds after the rank-deficient neighborhood is removed. All pieces and
all signs are restored at finite phase; no positive-volume or closing piece
is presumed absent.

## 3. Push the actual product coordinate, retaining both endpoint times

For the auxiliary sum S of **individual** diamond volumes use precisely
FL5; its primitive threshold is `sum (s_i positive)^q <= w^q`, together with
at least one positive phase. The physical interval is still the projected
union; section 6 will apply the already-proved physical-volume comparison.

Integrate both endpoint times first. Their measure is T-tau, not T. The
source x/z measure is one, the spectator source measure is 10^p, and
`d tau dX dZ = dr du dv/(2 tau)`. Integrating every spectator displacement
therefore gives the exact factor

```math
\begin{aligned}
F_d(A)&=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^p}
 \frac{[T-\sqrt{A+|b|^2}]_+}{\sqrt{A+|b|^2}}\,db,\\
A&=\tfrac12+r+u^2+v^2+Y^2+W^2,\\
F_d(A)&=\frac{(T^2-A)^{p/2+1}}2
 \int_{|z|\le1}
 \frac{1-|z|^2}
 {\sqrt{A+(T^2-A)|z|^2}\,[T+\sqrt{A+(T^2-A)|z|^2}]}\,dz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H4}
```

The last formula holds for A<T^2 and shows that F_d is smooth on a fixed
neighborhood of A=1/2, with F_d(1/2)>0. It retains the entire closing fibre
and both time measures. The displacement support is inside the ten-period
fundamental cube. All constants are from actual metric volume.

Localize smoothly near Y=W=0 and r=u=v=0, using even cutoffs in Y,W which
are one near zero. The complement was dealt with above. For h=2YW the exact
pushforward of the transverse measure is shown below. Its second line is on
the neighborhood where the r,u,v cutoff is one; elsewhere that smooth cutoff
multiplies the coefficient too.

```math
\begin{aligned}
G(r,u,v,h)&=\int
 \Psi\bigl(r,u,v,Y,h/(2Y)\bigr)\,\frac{dY}{2|Y|},\\
G(r,u,v,h)&=-F_d(\tfrac12+r+u^2+v^2)\log|h|
                 +\text{higher conormal terms and a smooth part}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H5}
```

Here Psi includes F_d and the actual even cutoffs; its compact Y,W support
bounds the displayed integral away from Y=0 for each h nonzero. The coefficient
of the logarithm is **one**, since the two source signs give
`integral dY/(2|Y|) = log(constant/|h|)`. There are separately two reflection
centers, 0 and 5; neither is the second source sign of this calculation.

For clarity, the needed error statement in (H5) is derived, not an assumed
conormal expansion. For a smooth even amplitude in Y and W, repeated use of

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(Y,W)&=\Psi(Y,0)+\Psi(0,W)-\Psi(0,0)
                      +Y^2W^2\Psi_1(Y,W)
\end{aligned}
\tag{H6}
```

gives, after pushing h=2YW, a smooth even part in h, a logarithm with coefficient
minus Psi(0,0), then h^2 times the same type of integral with smooth even
amplitude Psi_1. The identity follows by two applications of the integral
Taylor remainder. On a fixed support box the one-axis terms integrate
explicitly; subtracting their value at zero leaves a smooth function of h^2.
Iteration gives even powers h^(2j) times logarithms, plus a remainder.
Differentiating its integral k times gives a bound
`C |h|^(-k)(1+|log|h||)` before its h^(2j) prefactor. Iterating far enough
therefore makes the remaining term C5, uniformly with the needed r,u,v
derivatives. This supplies the finite expansion and derivative bounds used
next, without assuming analyticity of a boundary or omitting an axis.

Changing to the four phases divides the measure by sixteen. Since
`log|h|=log|lambda dot s|-log 4`, the latter constant only changes a smooth
amplitude. The two reflection centers and all source measures give the
leading log amplitude

```math
\begin{aligned}
-\mathcal B_d\log|\lambda\cdot s|,
\qquad \mathcal B_d=\frac{10^p}{8}F_d(1/2)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H7}
```

This is the amplitude of the whole physical pair measure in phase space,
not four separately counted physical pairs. Smooth cutoffs in this calculation
are one on the singular set; all their complements remain in the regular
localization and in the subsequent matching.

## 4. Full active-sign matching of the quartic logarithm

We give the elementary pushforward calculation because the four-positive
sector alone gives the **wrong coefficient**. Let s range in a neighborhood
of zero in four dimensions, lambda=(1,-1,-1,1), and let the pair measure have
smooth compact amplitude a(s) times `log|lambda dot s|`. Its causal primitive
uses every nonempty set I of positive coordinates; all the other coordinates
are negative. In that sector integrate the negative coordinates first.

An m-fold antiderivative of log|z| has its logarithmic part
`z^m log|z|/m!`; the remaining part is a polynomial. Integration by parts in
each negative coordinate is legitimate because the first antiderivative is
locally absolutely continuous, the amplitudes are smooth compactly supported,
and the original logarithm is locally integrable. Each inactive coordinate
has a nonzero lambda coefficient. The all-zero boundary of the inactive
coordinates contributes

```math
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a(s_I,0)}{m!\prod_{j\notin I}\lambda_j}
 \left(\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i s_i\right)^m
 \log\left|\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i s_i\right|,
\qquad m=4-|I|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H8}
```

Every remaining term has at least one inactive variable integrated against
a differentiated amplitude. Integrate that variable again. A new endpoint
term has an antiderivative of order at least m+1; otherwise repeat until the
remaining primitive has at least six derivatives integrated into it. That
remaining compact integral is C4 in the positive variables. Thus it contributes
an integer polynomial through degree four and an O(w^5) remainder after
scaling the positive variables. Endpoint terms of higher antiderivative
order start at w^5 log w. The same argument applies to the higher h powers
in (H5)--(H6). Taylor expansion of a(s_I,0) in (H8) shows that only a(0)
contributes at order w^4 log w. All estimates are dominated by integrable
powers times the logarithm of a nonzero linear form on a fixed q-ball.
This proves, with the full sign-sector sum, an integer polynomial through
degree four, the logarithm below, and an O(w^5(1+|log w|)) remainder.

Write B_q^k for the positive k-dimensional q-ball and introduce its **ordinary**
moments

```math
\begin{aligned}
J_q(a_1,\ldots,a_k)
 &=\int_{B_q^k}\prod_i u_i^{a_i}\,du
 =\frac{\prod_i\Gamma((a_i+1)/q)}
 {q^k\Gamma(1+\sum_i(a_i+1)/q)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H9}
```

Rescaling (H8) and summing **all fifteen nonempty sign sectors** gives the
coefficient of `a(0) w^4 log w`:

```math
\begin{aligned}
C(q)&=J_q(0,0,0,0)-4J_q(1,0,0)\\
 &\quad-2J_q(2,0)+6J_q(1,1)+\tfrac16.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H10}
```

The first term is the four-positive corner. Each of the four three-positive
sectors contributes minus J_q(1,0,0). The two same-sign positive pairs and
the four opposite-sign pairs give the next two terms. Each one-positive
sector contributes `(1/6) integral_0^1 u^3 du=1/24`. These are the regular
reference strips and lower sectors that an isolated corner calculation
would miss. Their sum is not zero.

### Prove the sign without numerical fitting

Take auxiliary independent random variables Z_i on the positive half-line
with density `q z^(q-1) exp(-z^q)`. They are used only to prove an ordinary
integral identity, not to replace the Poisson law. Their kth moment is
`m_k=Gamma(1+k/q)`. Expanding the fourth power and using (H9) proves

```math
\begin{aligned}
C(q)&=\frac{\mathbb E[(Z_1+Z_2-Z_3-Z_4)^4]}{24\Gamma(1+4/q)}
 >0,\qquad q>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H11}
```

For example the numerator divided by 24 is
`m_1^4-2m_2 m_1^2-(2/3)m_3 m_1+(3/2)m_2^2+m_4/6`, exactly (H10) times its
common gamma denominator. Positivity is strict because the density is
nondegenerate and all fourth moments are finite. Thus neither an interval
arithmetic guess nor a fitted coefficient supplies the sign.

Restoring the regular complement from section 2 and the sign in (H7) gives
the actual sum-volume long primitive

```math
\begin{aligned}
N_S^\delta(w)&=\sum_{j=1}^4 b_jw^j
 -\mathcal B_d C(q)w^4\log w
 +O\bigl(w^5(1+|\log w|)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{H12}
```

All b_j are finite actual compact-integral coefficients. Changing the unit
inside the logarithm changes b_4, not the logarithmic coefficient. No inverse
positive Taylor phase or vanishing signed moment has been used to manufacture
this expansion.

## 5. Evaluate the signed response with the actual dimensional kernel

For d>=6, the actual primitive roots include 1,2,3,4. The polynomial in
(H12) is therefore annihilated, with the fixed-phase tails retained as in G5.
Let n=floor(d/2) and set

```math
\begin{aligned}
R_n&=\prod_{k=5}^{n+1}(1-4/k)>0,\\
\left.\frac{d}{dr}M_d(r-1)\right|_{r=4}
 &=\frac{\Gamma(4/q)}{4q}R_n,\\
L_{w^4\log w}(\rho)
 &=-\frac{\beta_d}{q}c_d^{-4/q}\Gamma(4/q)R_n
                  \rho^{1-3/q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H13}
```

The empty product is one. These formulas are derivatives of G7's absolutely
convergent signed Mellin integral, not an analytic continuation of a divergent
integral. Terms differentiating density powers vanish because the pure
fourth mode is a root; thus no leading log rho is left in the signed response.
Multiplying the negative coefficient in (H12) by (H13) gives the positive
constant

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_d&=\mathcal B_d C(q)
 \frac{\beta_d}{q}c_d^{-4/q}\Gamma(4/q)R_n>0,\\
L_S^\delta(\rho)&=\mathcal D_d\rho^{1-6/d}
       +O\bigl(\rho^{1-8/d}(1+\log\rho)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{H14}
```

To justify the remainder, apply the exact signed Stieltjes formula to (H12),
continue the primitive constantly beyond its compact support, and subtract
the polynomial and a smoothly truncated logarithmic model. On its small
phase support the error is bounded as displayed. Rescale w by
`(c_d rho)^(-1/q)` in the derivative-kernel integral. The moments of
`z^(q+4) |K_d'(z^q)| (1+|log z|)` are finite, giving (H14); fixed positive
phase errors are exponentially small. In d=6 the error tends to zero. In
every d>=7 it is little-o of the displayed leading growth, even when it does
not itself tend to zero. No error is claimed to vanish merely by taking an
absolute value before signed cancellation.

its four-route transition or these dimensions from G-SS would change the
contract; no such exclusion is made.

1. **Every flat G-SS region in each of dimensions 2,3,4** has the canonical
   deterministic and expected joint limit, by the companion's full primitive,

   The precise complete behavior is (H15); the law, action and independent

4. The merged all-dimensional Minkowski, thin-torus and first-cut results
   remain valid under their original hypotheses. This reflection quotient
   is not a counterexample to any of those theorems.
