## 2. An actual remaining geometry, not an abstract missing estimate

Use flat three-dimensional null coordinates with
$`ds^2=du\,dv-dy^2`$ and volume $`du\,dv\,dy/2`$. Let
$`a=(0,-1,0)`$, $`b=(0,0,0)`$, $`c=(1,1,0)`$ and define

```math
\begin{aligned}
M_*&=I^+(a)\cap I^-(c)\setminus J^+(b),\\
f(v,y)&=\frac{y^2}{v+1},\qquad g_c(v,y)=1-\frac{y^2}{1-v},\\
g(v,y)&=\begin{cases}
 g_c(v,y),&-1\lt v\lt0\text{ or }(v=0,\ y\ne0),\\
 0,&v=y=0,\\
 \min\{y^2/v,g_c(v,y)\},&0\lt v\lt1,
 \end{cases}\\
M_*&=\{-1\lt v\lt1,\quad f(v,y)\lt u\lt g(v,y)\},\\
|M_*|&=c_3(2^{3/2}-1),\qquad c_3=\pi/12.
\end{aligned}\tag{SE1}
```

The lower set is future; both upper sets are past. Thus every closed ambient
interval between its endpoints stays in the open bounded region. It is globally
hyperbolic by ambient diamond compactness and containment. Its faces are null,
not timelike walls. The volume is the larger diamond minus the contained
$`I(b,c)`$; this volume identity is **not** bilocal additivity.

The unresolved local geometry is visible without a cutoff: for $`0\lt v\lt1`$
the two cones bound a cusp of width $`y^2/[v(v+1)]`$ along
$`K=\{u=y=0,0\le v\le1\}`$. At b a genuine cone apex enters. At
$`d=(0,1,0)`$ the additional upper cone closes the cusp; its same-side
crease is $`y^2=v(1-v)`$, $`u=1-v`$. The lower tip a is separate. The
regular mixed joint with the outer cone is the usual diamond screen except
for one tangent incidence. There is no finite smooth-sheet extension through b,
and the tube of K has critical codimension two. This is neither a finite
polyhedron, a strict-floor contact nor the round bicone of NG8.

For any fixed smooth positive conformal density the domain and containment
remain exactly SE1, while the physical intervals and both measures change.
Choose one physical temporal cutoff, for example a smooth one supported below
$`\delta=1/16`$ and equal to one below $`\delta/2`$. NG1 gives the four
ordered sectors for a fixed auxiliary tube field $`e_s`$ near K. Equivalently,
sum the **source** labels first, leaving target fields $`h_s,e_s`$, their
point weights, and all past partners. No member of either cross sector or
cutoff band is removed. The required estimate is NG2 for this actual domain,
including a geometrically derived singular coefficient if one survives.

## 3. Route one: joint anisotropic resolution and the lost end constant

For $`0\lt p_v\lt q_v\lt1`$, in the portion where the upper cone from b

```math
\begin{aligned}

\frac{y_q}{y_p}&=\frac{q_v}{p_v}
 \left(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{q_v-p_v}{q_v(p_v+1)}}\right)

\end{aligned}\tag{SE2}
```

The larger root grows as $`2q_v/p_v`$ as the source approaches b; the smaller
root tends to $`(q_v+1)/2`$. Thus NG10's compact-strip partner-comparability
constant is not uniform at the end. These are actual allowed pairs: take
$`y_p=p_v z`$, $`y_q=q_v z`$ with small fixed nonzero z, then choose both
inward normal distances with sum smaller than $`B/(q_v-p_v)`$. Both endpoint
masks hold for sufficiently small z. A far endpoint cannot be omitted from
the end neighborhood merely because its transverse size is different.

Resolve b jointly by
$`v=RV,\ y=R\theta Y,\ u=R\theta^2 U`$. The metric becomes
$`R^2\theta^2(dU\,dV-dY^2)`$, the lower graph becomes

$`\rho(R\theta)^3`$ and the three-dimensional action scale is

physical cutoffs: the original temporal gap is

## 4. Route two: a complete signed fibre theorem, including both cusp ends

There is a useful larger restricted result. Fix $`d\gt2`$, flat Minkowski
metric, a point a, and any open past set P containing a such that
$`M=I^+(a)\cap P`$ is bounded. No transversality, finite sheet representation,
finite contact order or continuity of the null exit radius is required.
For every q in M, **all its past partners in the volume integral** form
$`I(a,q)`$ up to its volume-null bounding cones and endpoints: every such
interior point is in P by the past-set property and in the future of a.
This is equality of continuum integrals, not a change to the closed discrete
order or removal of any positive-volume partner neighborhood. This stronger
fibre fact, not causal convexity alone, licenses the time reversal of ND2–ND6.

Let $`R(\omega)`$ be the first exit radius of the null ray
$`a+(r,r\omega)`$ from P. Openness and boundedness give fixed positive lower
and finite upper bounds on R; R is measurable. Define geometric data

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J_a(P)&=\int_{S^{d-2}}R(\omega)^{d-2}\,d\omega,\\
\mathcal J_a(P;\chi)&=(d-2)\int_{S^{d-2}}\int_0^{R(\omega)}
 \chi(a+(r,r\omega))r^{d-3}\,dr\,d\omega.
\end{aligned}\tag{SE3}
```

These are null-ray lengths and their actual weighted ray measure, defined
before an action limit. On regular exit screens the usual Gram determinant
identifies the unweighted functional with induced screen area. No area is
assigned to an unproved singular trace.

**Flat common-past-tip theorem.** The complete deterministic action tends to
SE3; with a smooth target field it tends to the second line. Separately the
original-law expectation has the same limit. Here is the complete remainder
argument. Let $`B_d`$ be ND4–ND5's genuine **signed-integrated** response.
For $`d\gt2`$ it is nonnegative with the finite mass ND6. The exact point and
all-past-partner sum is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_{\rho,d}[1,\chi;M]
 &=\rho^{2/d}\int_M\chi(q)B_d(c_d\rho\sigma(a,q)^{d/2})\,dq,\\
W_\chi(s)&=\int_{S^{d-2}}\int_0^{B_*}
 \frac{r^{d-2}\chi(a+(\sqrt{r^2+s},r\omega))}
 {2\sqrt{r^2+s}}
 \mathbf1_P(a+(\sqrt{r^2+s},r\omega))\,dr\,d\omega,\\
\mathcal A_{\rho,d}[1,\chi;M]
 &=c_d^{-2/d}\int_0^\infty B_d(z^{d/2})
             W_\chi(z/(c_d\rho)^{2/d})\,dz
 \longrightarrow\mathcal J_a(P;\chi).
\end{aligned}\tag{SE4}
```

For each fixed r strictly below R, the indicator tends to one by openness.
Above R it is zero: membership of the timelike-raised point would force
membership of its causal past null-ray point. The r=R value has zero radial
measure even if R is discontinuous on an arbitrary angular set. The majorant
$`\|\chi\|_\infty r^{d-3}/2`$ is integrable. Hence W is bounded and right
continuous at zero, without a derivative of R. ND6 and dominated convergence
prove the full critically normalized remainder, not just a coefficient.
This extends ND's **stated geometric scope**; it reuses its proved fibre
response, not its transverse-screen theorem. Higher d uses the actual beta
mixture, not a Gaussian extrapolated from d=3 or 4.

Apply this to SE1. The point a is in its past set. Except in the single null
direction from a through b, the first exit is the outer diamond screen; in
that direction it is b. Since $`d=3`$ that one angular value changes no
SE3 integral. The independently induced outer screen has circumference
$`\pi\sqrt2`$ (proper diameter $`\sqrt2`$). Thus

```math
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\mathcal A_{\rho,3}(M_*)&=\pi\sqrt2,\\
\sup_{\rho\gt0}|\mathcal A_{\rho,3}[1,e_s;M_*]|&\le Cs.
\end{aligned}\tag{SE5}
```

For the second line use a bounded tube field around K, away from a. Its
spatial radial coordinate about a is bounded below, and its angular support

uniform bound. It contains both cusp ends, both signs of v near b, the cap

For all members of this theorem, bounded Borel geometry, atomless volume,

## 6. Route three: an actual fractional coefficient of the intrinsic union

## 7. A complete intrinsic closing threshold, with both matched tails

Keep NP1's all-null product geometry, now for every fixed
$`0\lt T\lt L/2`$. All four faces, two same-side seams, two NN screens,

The actual endpoint weight has a non-canceling representation. Put
$`\delta_T=T-U`$ and $`v=U+\delta_T z`$. For the bracket in NP3,

```math
\begin{aligned}
B_T(v)&=(T^2+v^2)\log(T/v)-T^2+v^2\\
 &=(T-v)^3\int_0^1(1-t)^2
       \left[\frac1{v+(T-v)t}+\frac{v^2}{(v+(T-v)t)^3}\right]dt,\\
G_T(U)&=(T-U)^{9/2}L_T(U),\\
L_T(T)&=\frac{256\pi T\sqrt{2T}}{945}\gt0.
\end{aligned}\tag{IF2}
```

The first identity is Taylor's integral formula using
$`\partial_T^3 B_T(v)=2/T+2v^2/T^3`$ and three zero initial coefficients.
Insert it in NP3 and set v as above. The remaining z integral has the fixed
weight $`z^{1/2}(1-z)^3`$, times a smooth positive function. This proves
