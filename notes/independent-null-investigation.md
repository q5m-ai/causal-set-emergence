## 2. Reconstruct the observable before choosing its representation

For the selected closed ambient order, let V be the volume of the whole
physical exclusive interval. Containment identifies this with the restricted
interval; null boundaries have zero continuum measure without changing the
discrete order. Both endpoint measures occur in

```math
\begin{aligned}
P_\rho(M)&=\int_{p\preceq q\in M}K_d(\rho V(p,q))\,d\mu(p)d\mu(q),\
\mathcal A_{\rho,d}(M)&=a_d\rho^{2/d}\mu(M)
                 -\beta_d\rho^{1+2/d}P_\rho(M),\
\mathbb E A^{\mathrm{disc}}_{\rho,d}(M)&=\mathcal A_{\rho,d}(M).
\end{aligned}\tag{IN1}
```

The last equality is a **separate finite-density** Mecke argument: Borel order
and interval relation, atomless finite volume, both intensities, and the
original exclusive-layer factorials. Each layer is bounded by the square of
the Poisson cardinality. It gives no deterministic asymptotic or sample-wise
convergence. No probability limit interchange is needed or inferred here.
The constants and kernels are exactly #90/dimension-kernels; no kernel is
replaced by an exponential.

Use one fixed positive physical temporal cutoff throughout. Its short and
long masks sum to one; only short receives the point. For any endpoint
partition, every ordered product is summed and the diagonal products sum to
one. This remains true in every formula below. Intermediate-point unions
are formed **before** K, never by summing route kernels.

### Necessity audit

IU's averaging identity follows by transferring the Euler derivatives onto
the exponential. The adjoint for ordinary volume integration, the simple
Mellin zeros and the even-dimensional logarithmic point term give precisely
GT1–GT4. Constant continuation of the physical primitive, and full-half-line
continuation of the point subtraction, retain the upper tails. Thus, **if**
independent geometric correction data and a target have first been constructed,
GT2's complete signed remainder is equivalent to their action asymptotic.
This is an algebraic equivalence, not a producer or a corrected theorem.

GT7/GT10 concern only a particular long disintegration. A pointwise divided
increment bound, an absolute majorant, or a separate long trace is sufficient
when it actually yields the complete GT2 remainder. None is established as
necessary for the original task. A zero uncorrected increment would be the
**wrong target** on the spherical family. Reading “control the increment” as
only “compute the whole signed response after restoration” makes it a valid
reformulation, but no longer a distinguished geometric estimate. No admitted

specified complete action asymptotic holds; the equivalence forbids that.

GT5–GT9's optical comparison, almost-everywhere regular arrival argument,

## 3. Attack I: transport a future cap after integrating all past partners

Choose a smooth temporal coordinate with volume `j(c,z) dc dσ(z)` and set
`M_c=M intersect {t<c}`. Time monotonicity proves containment for M_c; it
introduces no new interval phase. For a target in M_c its whole past partner
set in M is already below c. Define the complete target response and cap flux:

```math
\begin{aligned}
R_\rho(q)&=\rho^{2/d}\left[a_d-\beta_d\rho
       \int_{M\cap J^-(q)}K_d(\rho V(p,q))\,d\mu(p)\right],\
\mathcal A_{\rho,d}(M_c)&=\int_{M_c}R_\rho(q)\,d\mu(q),\
\frac{d}{dc}\mathcal A_{\rho,d}(M_c)
 &=\int_Y\mathbf1_M(q(c,z))R_\rho(q(c,z))j(c,z)\,d\sigma(z)
                      \quad\text{for almost every }c.
\end{aligned}\tag{IN2}
```

**Proof:** K is bounded, so at each finite density all integrals are absolutely
integrable. Fubini and the fundamental theorem for indefinite integrals give
the last line. The point and all past partners have been combined **before**
transport. There is no factor two for these ordered pairs, no surface-density
substitution for j, and no reciprocal projected area. At seams/closing caps
the actual target indicator remains in the flux. Restoring the two fixed
physical masks and all endpoint labels commutes with this finite-density
calculation. The cap is an integration device, not a density-dependent cutoff.

For `M=F intersect P`, with F future and P past, the inner domain is exactly
`F intersect I^-(q)` up to null boundaries. It is a diamond only when F really
has the common-tip property. This makes the proposed bypass concrete: derive
a signed transport law for that **complete** response, then integrate the
future cap. A smooth-test-function local wave-operator limit cannot be applied
to the discontinuous indicator of an arbitrary characteristic F without a
new estimate. Smoothing it first changes the source; the required density-
uniform error does not follow from fixed-density dominated convergence.

### First attempted closure: a bounded signed-measure limit

One might try a density-uniform total-variation bound on `Rρ dμ`, identify its
weak limit geometrically, and pair it with the cap indicator. This is too
strong **even for an actual flat diamond**. In null coordinates take
`0<u,v<T`, density one-half, dimension two and `Z=ρuv/2`. Directly integrating
the real K2 over all past partners gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
R_\rho(u,v)&=2\rho(1-Z)e^{-Z},\
A&=\rho T^2/2,\
\int_M R_\rho\,d\mu&=2(1-e^{-A})\longrightarrow2,\
\int_M |R_\rho|\,d\mu
 &=2\int_0^A|1-z|e^{-z}\log(A/z)\,dz
   =\frac4e\log A+O(1).
\end{aligned}\tag{IN3}
```

To verify the partner integral, its density in Z is `log(Z/z)` and its
value times rho is `[1-(1-Z)exp(-Z)]/2`. Integrating the resulting response
in u and then v proves the third line. The final coefficient follows from
`integral |1-z| exp(-z) dz=2/e`; its logarithmic moment is absolutely finite.
This independently rederives DL's signed/absolute distinction, rather than
announcing DL as a new theorem. The independent target is the two NN points
with counting measure, curvature zero. The result is a successful complete
action, **not** an obstruction to that target.

Consequently bounded-measure compactness is not a general necessity. Smooth
or signed testing can work without it. One must still control pairing with
an actual cap, not just with smooth test functions. This does not prove
unbounded variation of the *already spatially integrated* cap flux; further
cancellation in that flux remains a viable route. We therefore continued
with an exact integration of both endpoints rather than stopping at IN3.

## 4. Attack II: an inverse-free arbitrary-metric null suspension

Let Y be any closed connected smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension n,
put `d=n+2`, and use the fixed product spacetime and region

```math
\begin{aligned}
g&=dt^2-dz^2-h_Y,\
M_T&=\{(t,z,y):|t|+|z|\lt T/2,\ y\in Y\},\
\mu(M_T)&=\tfrac12T^2\mathrm{Vol}(Y),\
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{reg}}(M_T)
 &=\tfrac14T^2\int_Y R_Y\,d\mu_Y+2\mathrm{Vol}(Y).
\end{aligned}\tag{IN4}
```

This is not a proposed restriction of #156. It is an **unambiguously admitted
subproblem** of its arbitrary-metric null requirement, with no assumed route
count, no finite type and no exclusion of cut or focal sets. The spatial
product is complete; the ambient ultrastatic spacetime is globally hyperbolic.
The functions t±z are monotone on every future causal curve, proving closed
ambient containment. The region is precompact. Its four null faces, two
same-side seams and two distinct-ray NN screens are precisely NP's boundary
types. The screens have induced metric h_Y. The scalar curvature is R_Y in
#90's sign convention. These independently prove the last line; no extra
seam coefficient is assumed absent from the *actual* answer.

### Actual physical phase from metric triangle data

Let r and s be distances in Y from an intermediate x to endpoints y and y'.
Boost only the flat (t,z) block and put `u=sqrt(tau²-z²)`. The whole ambient
interval at zero flat separation has volume W. Time Fubini gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
W(u;y,y')&=\int_Y\int_{\mathbb R}
 [u-\sqrt{r^2+\zeta^2}-\sqrt{s^2+\zeta^2}]_+\,d\zeta\,d\mu_Y(x),\
B(u;r,s)&=\frac{\sqrt{(u^2-(r+s)^2)(u^2-(r-s)^2)}}{2u}
                                 \quad(u\gt r+s),\
W_u(u;y,y')&=\int_{r+s\lt u}2B(u;r,s)\,d\mu_Y(x).
\end{aligned}\tag{IN5}
```

The integrand is a union of physical points, not of named geodesics. Multiple
minimizers affect the distances, not the multiplicity of x. At fixed r,s the
inner integral equals `u B-r² asinh(B/r)-s² asinh(B/s)` on its active set,
with the continuous zero conventions. Its derivative is 2B; dominated
convergence on bounded u intervals proves IN5 also across opening. Equivalently,
push the *physical* measure of x to `(a,b)=(r+s,r-s)`. Then W_u is its Abel
integral with weight `sqrt((u²-a²)(u²-b²))/u` on a<u. No smooth density of this
pushforward is assumed. This is a geometric coarea alternative, not GT7's
inverse-area coarea.

Attempting to average this conditional triangle measure over y,y' **before**
applying K loses correlations: K of the average W is not the average of K(W).
Already the finite-density quadratic coefficient has a nonzero variance
error whenever W varies. Thus ordinary averaged triangle-volume bounds do
not close the argument. What is needed is a signed theorem for this nonlinear
Abel potential, retaining its endpoint-conditional data.

### Both endpoint integrations and the physical cutoff

The source (t,z) overlap is exactly
`(T-tau-z)_+(T-tau+z)_+/2`. The change
`tau=u cosh(eta), z=u sinh(eta)` has Jacobian u and range
`|eta|<log(T/u)`. Its complete integral produces

```math
\begin{aligned}
G_T(u)&=u[(T^2+u^2)\log(T/u)-T^2+u^2],\
F_\rho(u)&=\int_{Y^2}\mathbf1_{d_Y(y,y')\lt u}
                  K_d(\rho W(u;y,y'))\,d\mu_Y(y)d\mu_Y(y'),\
P_\rho(M_T)&=\int_0^T G_T(u)F_\rho(u)\,du,\
\mathcal A_{\rho,d}(M_T)
 &=\tfrac12a_d\rho^{2/d}T^2\mathrm{Vol}(Y)
       -\beta_d\rho^{1+2/d}\int_0^T G_T(u)F_\rho(u)\,du.
\end{aligned}\tag{IN6}
```

This is a complete observable, not an isolated long sector. The two Y
measures are not normalized to probability. At `0<delta<T/2`, the sharp
physical-time short mask gives exactly

```math
\begin{aligned}
G_{T,\mathrm{s}}^\delta(u)&=\mathbf1_{u\lt\delta}\,u
 \left[(T^2+u^2)\,\mathrm{arcosh}(\delta/u)
                         -2T\sqrt{\delta^2-u^2}\right],\
G_{T,\mathrm{l}}^\delta&=G_T-G_{T,\mathrm{s}}^\delta.
\end{aligned}\tag{IN7}
```

Indeed the rapidity bound for short is `arcosh(delta/u)`, smaller than the
physical closing bound because `2 delta<T`. A smooth physical cutoff is the
ordinary fixed-band average of this identity. In particular **u<delta alone
is not short**: the rapidity restriction must remain. Point goes to short
once. Every pair, cap and complement is retained; the auxiliary boost does
not change the physical cutoff. Finite ordered partitions may be summed
before this reduction but cannot be discarded term by term.

### A real gain, and a second failed closure

There is a simple external-width Green equation. Write `D=T ∂T`. G_T and
its first two T derivatives vanish at T=u, its third derivative there is
four, and its interior expression is killed by `D²(D-2)²`. Therefore

```math
\begin{aligned}
D^2(D-2)^2P_\rho(M_T)&=4T^4F_\rho(T),\
D^2(D-2)^2\mathcal A_{\rho,d}(M_T)
 &=-4\beta_d\rho^{1+2/d}T^4F_\rho(T),\
\ker[D^2(D-2)^2]&=\mathrm{span}\{1,\log T,T^2,T^2\log T\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{IN8}
```

These identities hold distributionally on positive T and classically where
F is continuous. F is bounded at every finite density, so differentiation of
the Volterra integral is justified without a smooth phase. No inverse of W
or GT10 increment appears. G_T is positive and has cubic closing order;
this is actual smoothing by **both endpoint measures**, not a density jet.

But trying to solve the asymptotic problem by the differentiated equation
alone loses *both* the bulk T² term and the constant screen term in IN4.
It is not legitimate to differentiate an unproved density-limit expansion,
or to commute the small-width boundary data with the density limit.
The actual n=0 flat diamond (Y one point, d=2) makes the failure decisive:

```math
\begin{aligned}
W(u)&=u^2/2,\
\mathcal A_{\rho,2}(M_T)&=2(1-e^{-\rho T^2/2}),\
D^2(D-2)^2\mathcal A_{\rho,2}(M_T)
 &=-16\rho^2T^4K_2(\rho T^2/2)\longrightarrow0\quad(T\gt0),\
\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\mathcal A_{\rho,2}(M_T)&=2\ne0.
\end{aligned}\tag{IN9}
```

At each fixed density the small-T action tends to zero; its limiting constant
does not. The exact integral from zero to fixed T retains the missing boundary
layer. No geometry or physical cutoff is varied with density in this example.
Thus the new route has a precise two-part obligation: control the signed
interior response **and** determine the homogeneous modes from matched origin
and boundary data. An equation away from zero, first-order convergence or an
absolute bound cannot substitute for that matching. This is not another
proof of the diamond theorem: it is an admitted falsifier of that inference.

## 5. Adversarial checks: different mechanisms must give different answers

```math
\end{aligned}\tag{IN10}
```

The volume in IN5 becomes O4 after the same flat boost and physical Fubini.

For the stronger IU8 remainder, the radial difference is O(r), while only
